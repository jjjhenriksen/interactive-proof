Interactive Proof turns a dense mathematical paper into a guided learning space.

We start with a theorem: the first n odd numbers sum to n squared. The paper and its Lean formalization stay side by side.

I can select any passage and open More details. This explanation is generated live by GPT-5.6 through the Responses API. The app supplies the selected paper block, its mapped Lean declaration, glossary, prerequisites, and recent context. The model writes the explanation; the app owns citations and trust labels.

Notice the rendered Markdown and equations. If I want to check the answer, Sources and formal proof leads back to the exact paper passage and Lean code. The Lean package is separately build-verified, with the revision and toolchain recorded in the expandable proof details.

I can change the explanation depth or ask a follow-up without losing the original passage. Uploaded papers and Lean files are temporary and clearly marked unverified; the repository-backed sample is the only verified package.

Codex was the engineering collaborator. It helped turn the hackathon requirements into the product plan, architecture, tests, accessibility pass, deployment, and release review. GPT-5.6 is the runtime explanation engine.

Interactive Proof makes the step that stopped your reading teachable, without pretending that an AI explanation is a formal proof.
